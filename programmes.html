/ Results-Chain Governance /

How We Measure & Govern Every Programme

To maintain institutional credibility with development partners, government ministries, and communities, GNEP Africa applies a strict five-stage results chain and never infers long-term ecological impact from activity counts alone.

Accountability Centre
Organizations, state ministries, universities, and foundations can partner with GNEP Africa as Implementation Partners (field tree restoration and school climate clubs), Workshop Co-Hosts (civil society capacity building), or Founding Sponsors for planned initiatives such as the Umaru Musa Fellowship Initiative (UMFI).
Contributors can collaborate on specific programme areas such as school climate education, community agroforestry, Hausa climate translation, or policy research briefs—allowing you to direct your technical expertise or institutional network to the area you care about most.
Direct outputs (such as 150 economic tree seedlings planted, 20 CSO representatives trained, and 17 publications released) are reported with exact dates and partner credits, while medium-term outcomes (seedling survival rates and legislative uptake) are tracked through 90-day and annual follow-up audits.
In compliance with GNEP's public accountability standards, initiatives in active design or partner mobilisation (such as the Umaru Musa Fellowship Initiative and the Katsina Economic Linkage & Capability Model) are explicitly badged as Planned / Future Initiatives until field operations formally commence.
Results Chain Stage Institutional Definition GNEP Africa Verified Example Verification Standard
1. Inputs Human, technical, partner, and material resources committed to an intervention. Facilitator expertise, nursery seedlings, training manuals, co-host venue with AYINET & The Green Minds Space. Internal programme records & partner agreements.
2. Activities Actions and field operations directly executed by GNEP or co-hosts. Conducting field planting drives, convening CSO policy brief drafting sessions, publishing policy analyses. Dated event logs & authenticated field photography.
3. Outputs Direct, countable products and immediate deliverables of an activity. 150 economic tree seedlings planted; 20 CSO representatives trained; 17 publications released. Attendance sheets, seedling counts, and repository DOIs/codes.
4. Outcomes Medium-term changes in participant behaviour, institutional practice, or seedling survival. Trained CSOs submitting evidence-based briefs to state ministries; planted seedlings surviving dry-season stress. Follow-up surveys and 90-day / annual field monitoring.
5. Long-Term Impact Durable ecological restoration, climate resilience, or statutory policy compliance. Micro-climate stabilization, improved sub-national budget transparency, and reduced community vulnerability. Requires multi-year longitudinal data and independent counterfactuals.
