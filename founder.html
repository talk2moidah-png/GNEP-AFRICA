Chapter 1

Executive Overview & Background

Mohammed Labaran Sani is a Nigerian climate policy researcher, development practitioner, and social entrepreneur whose work focuses on climate governance, adaptation finance, institutional effectiveness, and community-led environmental action in Africa.

With an academic foundation in Political Science and postgraduate training in Climate Change Policy, he works at the intersection of research, public policy, and practical development. His professional journey spans education, public health programme support, entrepreneurship training, environmental advocacy, and youth leadership, reflecting a sustained interest in how institutions can translate policy commitments into meaningful outcomes for communities.

“Ambitious climate commitments mean little if they fail to produce corresponding improvements in the lives of the communities they are intended to protect. Our responsibility is to turn policy ambition into institutional delivery.”

He is the Founder and Project Lead of the Global Nature and Environmental Partnership (GNEP), a youth-led environmental initiative established to advance climate literacy, renewable energy awareness, sustainable livelihoods, and evidence-informed environmental governance. Through GNEP, he seeks to strengthen the participation of young people and civil society organisations in climate policy processes, particularly in underserved communities in Nigeria. His research examines the institutional and financial barriers that limit climate action across Africa, with particular attention to the implementation of national climate policies and the delivery of adaptation finance.

Currently pursuing a Master of Science in Climate Change Policy at the Pan-African University Institute for Water and Energy Sciences, including Climate Change (PAUWES), in Algeria, Sani is developing an interdisciplinary approach to African development challenges. His work connects political institutions, public policy, environmental sustainability, and the economic conditions that shape communities' capacity to respond to climate change.

Chapter 2

Academic Foundations and Intellectual Development

Sani's academic development began with an interest in political institutions, public administration, regional integration, and the processes through which governments make and implement decisions. He obtained a Diploma in Office Technology and Management Studies from Hassan Usman Katsina Polytechnic in Nigeria in 2015. The programme provided an early foundation in administration, organisation, communication, and information management, skills that would later support his work in programme coordination and institutional engagement.

He subsequently studied Political Science at Umaru Musa Yar'adua University in Katsina State, graduating in 2021 with a Bachelor of Science degree. His undergraduate studies examined the structures of political authority, public policy, international relations, political economy, development administration, and the relationship between institutions and society. These areas of study helped establish the analytical foundation for his subsequent interests in governance and development policy.

The African Union & Regional Integration Thesis

His undergraduate research focused on The African Union (AU), Regional Integration, and the Challenges of Globalisation in the Twenty-First Century. The study examined regional integration as a pathway for African cooperation and development while considering the challenges that shape the continent's position in an increasingly interconnected global system. The thesis, subsequently made available through Zenodo, reflects his early engagement with questions concerning African institutions, regional cooperation, and the implementation of collective development ambitions.

This initial research direction remains relevant to his present work. His transition from political science to climate policy reflects a continuing interest in the relationship between institutional commitments and practical outcomes. While his undergraduate research examined the African Union and regional integration, his current work considers how African governments and institutions can improve the delivery of climate policies, mobilise resources, and respond to the needs of vulnerable populations.

Sani is currently pursuing an MSc in Climate Change Policy at PAUWES in Tlemcen, Algeria, with completion expected in August 2027. His postgraduate studies extend his earlier interest in public policy into environmental governance, climate adaptation, mitigation planning, climate finance, and sustainable energy transitions. Through this training, he is developing the capacity to examine climate change as both an environmental challenge and a governance issue shaped by institutional performance, financing arrangements, and policy choices.

Chapter 3

Professional Experience and Community Engagement

Sani's professional experience spans education, public health, entrepreneurship development, and environmental action. These roles provided practical exposure to community realities, organisational coordination, and the challenges of programme delivery.

Between October 2021 and March 2024, he worked as a teacher at Shahrab International School in Katsina State. In this role, he was responsible for classroom instruction, curriculum support, and mentoring secondary school students. The experience reinforced his understanding of education as a foundation for individual development and social awareness. Observing the limited presence of climate and environmental issues in formal school curricula, he recognised the need to introduce young people to sustainability concepts early in their educational development.

Jobberman Nigeria (2023–2024)

Delivered soft-skills and entrepreneurship training reaching over 12,000 market women and young people across Kano, Kaduna, and Katsina states under the Mastercard Foundation Young Africa Works programme.

eHealth Africa (2024)

Provided digital data collection and technological field support for public health campaigns and childhood immunisation monitoring, ensuring rigorous data integrity.

Chapter 4

Founding the Global Nature and Environmental Partnership

In January 2025, Sani founded the Global Nature and Environmental Partnership (GNEP), a youth-led environmental organisation based in Katsina State, Nigeria. The initiative was established to address a persistent gap in African environmental action: the divide between climate policy discussions and community-level realities.

While international forums and national policy documents frequently articulate ambitious climate goals, local communities often have limited access to the information, resources, and institutional support needed to adapt to changing environmental conditions. GNEP was designed to help bridge this gap by translating environmental policy and scientific knowledge into accessible educational programmes and community-level initiatives.

Ground-Truthed Achievements under GNEP:

  • 20 Civil Society Representatives Trained: Workshop on policy brief writing, proposal development, elevator pitching, and ethical AI research tools in Katsina State.
    • Mohammed Labaran Sani facilitating the 2026 Civil Society Policy & Advocacy Workshop in Katsina State.
  • 150 Economic Tree Seedlings Planted: Joint initiative with AYINET to promote reforestation and agroforestry in northern Nigeria.
  • SUBEB & UNICEF Workshop: Active participation in institutional field work approaches for climate change and green skills (19 August 2026).
  • Climate Clubs in Schools: Establishing student-led environmental awareness networks across Katsina State schools.
Chapter 5

Research, Publications, and Policy Contributions

Research is an increasingly important component of Sani's professional trajectory. His work examines climate finance, institutional effectiveness, environmental governance, and the implementation of public policies in African contexts.

Policy Paper • Under Review 2026

How Debt Swaps Can Unlock Finance for Climate Adaptation in Africa

Author: Mohammed Labaran Sani

Examines the potential of debt-for-climate and debt-for-adaptation swaps to create fiscal space for vulnerable African nations, relieving sovereign debt distress while funding resilient infrastructure.

Working Paper In Development

Rethinking Climate Finance in Africa: Governance, Institutional Capacity, and Adaptation Outcomes

Author: Mohammed Labaran Sani

Considers the institutional and fiduciary conditions that influence the absorptive capacity and impact of multilateral climate finance on community-level resilience.

Comparative Study PAUWES Research

Institutional Determinants of Delayed Implementation of National Climate Plans: Comparative Evidence from Nigeria and Cameroon

Author: Mohammed Labaran Sani

A comparative institutional analysis exploring why nationally determined contributions (NDCs) encounter bureaucratic delays and policy inertia in Central and West Africa.

Spatial-Temporal Analysis PAUWES Research

Evaluating Climate Policy Effectiveness in Algeria and Nigeria: A Spatio-Temporal GHG Emissions Analysis (2000–2025)

Author: Mohammed Labaran Sani

Combines satellite data, GIS mapping, and policy timeline benchmarking to evaluate how national mitigation frameworks correlate with emission trajectories in two major oil-producing African states.

Chapter 6

Leadership, Service, and Professional Development

Sani's commitment to leadership and service predates his current environmental work. During his undergraduate studies, he participated actively in student governance and youth organisations. He served as Secretary-General of the National Union of Katsina State Students (NUKSS) in 2021, and held the position of Director of Socials within the Department of Political Science at Umaru Musa Yar'adua University in 2019.

During his National Youth Service Corps (NYSC) experience, he served as Secretary-General of the Agro-Allied Community Development Service (CDS) group in Katsina State. The role involved coordinating community-based agricultural and environmental initiatives, organising meetings, maintaining records, and facilitating collaboration among corps members and local stakeholders.

In 2021, Sani was honoured as an Outstanding Student Leader in the Department of Political Science at Umaru Musa Yar'adua University, acknowledging his exceptional dedication to student welfare and academic advocacy.

Chapter 7

Vision for African Climate Governance

Sani's long-term professional interests centre on improving the effectiveness of climate governance and public policy in Africa. He is particularly concerned with the institutional and financial conditions that determine whether climate commitments translate into practical adaptation, resilience, and sustainable development.

“For Sani, the future of African climate governance will depend partly on the ability of African researchers, policymakers, civil society organisations, and young leaders to develop solutions informed by evidence and grounded in the continent's institutional and social realities.”

His immediate research direction includes examining policy implementation and climate governance in West Africa. He aims to deepen his understanding of how public institutions coordinate climate action, how financing reaches intended beneficiaries, and how evidence can be used to improve programme design and accountability.

Through GNEP, he intends to continue developing opportunities for young researchers and civil society practitioners to participate in policy processes. One of his proposed initiatives is a climate policy practice platform that would bring researchers and civil society organisations together to address practical policy problems, develop evidence-based recommendations, and strengthen the capacity of local actors to engage with decision-makers.

For Mohammed Labaran Sani, the measure of progress lies in whether knowledge informs decisions, whether institutions fulfil their responsibilities, and whether the benefits of public policy reach the people for whom they are intended.
